What the plots mean & model conventions

Each person holds a probability mass function on a finite θ grid. The bars sum to one; these are not smoothed continuous densities. Connecting lines in the comparison plot are guides between grid points. The dashed vertical line marks the generating coin bias, not an estimated truth.

Every round uses shared Bayesian evidence, then synchronous social influence and cash settlement. A displayed state at round t follows the payments and contacts of round t − 1. Baseline edge labels are fixed. With the tanh law, a directed contact’s effective sign can change; the network lines continue to show its baseline relationship.

The active rule controls network glyphs, cash values, history, and topology comparisons. Checkboxes in the large PMF plot control only its overlaid curves. Zero-payment opponents still repel. Turning off public evidence gives a social-only diagnostic. Positive income adds external money and constrains spending by the income budget.

Initial beliefs, initial wealth, and evidence are paired across topologies; contacts are paired across payment rules within a topology. Random-regular degree is 2m. Complete graphs also change density. Changing the round horizon redraws part of the contact sequence; compare at the same horizon and seed. New full-belief runs on the control networks extend the previous cash-only experiments.

Only display frames may be sampled for long runs; every model round is simulated. Views show exact saved states without interpolation. Mini-plots use a fixed mass range 0–1, the selected-node plot uses the same fixed range across rules and time, and the history uses its labeled mass scale. These are exploratory realizations, not ensemble conclusions.
