    What the plots mean & model conventions

    The initialization controls create a new experiment and clear its intervention log. The agent editor changes a chosen person during that experiment. Expand the parameter groups below for meanings, units, and interactions.

    Network, initial conditions & reproducibility
    Network ensemble
    Choose one or more initial undirected graphs: Barabási–Albert grows a network by preferential attachment; random regular gives every agent the same degree and is sampled until connected; complete connects every pair. Initial beliefs, cash, traits, and evidence are shared across families, while graph, relationship-sign, and contact streams differ. Connection edits can subsequently change the graph family.
    Agents (N)
    Number of people in each network, with IDs 0 through N − 1. The explorer supports 4–100 agents. Each has its own belief distribution, cash balance, giving preference, and loyalty trait.
    Attachment m
    In the Barabási–Albert graph, each newly added node attaches to m existing nodes; m is a positive integer smaller than N. For random regular, each node has degree 2m, which must be smaller than N. Complete ignores m and has degree N − 1. BA and regular graphs do not have equal mean degree; their densities can differ substantially.
    Initial wealth distribution
    Uniform samples cash evenly between the wealth bounds. Loguniform samples log(cash) evenly between their logarithms, giving equal probability to equal multiplicative intervals. This affects only initial cash, not initial beliefs.
    Wealth minimum & maximum
    Bounds for initial cash in arbitrary nominal money units; require 0 < minimum < maximum. Later cash is not confined to these bounds. A run with no income or cash interventions conserves total cash, although individual balances change.
    Grid points (K)
    Number of equally spaced coin-bias hypotheses from θ = 0 to θ = 1, including both endpoints; spacing is 1/(K − 1). The explorer supports 5–51 points. Beliefs are probability masses that sum to one, not continuous densities. Increasing K changes the discrete model, as well as plot resolution.
    Initial beliefs
    Each agent starts from a Gaussian-shaped mass function on that grid. Centers are sampled uniformly from 0–1 and widths from 0.2–0.8, then masses are normalized by their sum. These ranges are fixed model conventions; the seed controls their draws. A Gaussian's input center and width need not equal its achieved mean and standard deviation on a bounded discrete grid.
    Rounds
    Number of synchronous model updates, from 1–1000 in the explorer. State 0 is the initial state; state t is after update t. Every round is computed. Long runs normally display at most 501 states, plus exact states just before and after each intervention; displayed states are never averaged or interpolated.
    Seed
    A nonnegative integer initializes separate random streams for graphs, beliefs, wealth, evidence, initial signs, traits, and contacts. A matched configuration and implementation reproduce the draws. Changing the number of rounds can change the contact sequence, so the same seed at a different horizon does not guarantee a shared prefix. Saved experiment JSON retains the exact inputs and model/dependency identity.
    Evidence, social learning & influence
    Shared coin evidence
    When enabled, every agent observes the same Bernoulli outcome each round and updates its beliefs before social influence: multiply masses by θ for heads or 1 − θ for tails, then normalize. When disabled, this Bayesian step is skipped; social learning, payments, income, and interventions still operate. An observed outcome with zero total probability raises an error.
    True bias θ*
    Probability of heads for the evidence-generating coin, between 0 and 1. It also locates the vertical truth marker and the reference for belief-error diagnostics. It does not set agents' initial beliefs. With evidence disabled, it has no effect on their dynamics.
    Social rate μ
    A dimensionless parameter strictly between 0 and 1 that sets resistance to social influence. Agent i's self-weight is sᵢ = degreeᵢ × reference payment × (1 − μ) / μ.Larger μ reduces self-weight and increases responsiveness at fixed degree and contact weights. It is not a probability of contacting someone or a fixed fraction mixed at each update. Connection edits update degree and therefore self-weight. An isolated agent has no social update.
    Reference payment (w₀)
    A positive money scale, in the same nominal units as cash and payments. It sets self-weight under both influence laws. Under attenuation it also sets the unpaid-opponent repulsion scale and the payment scale that weakens repulsion. It does not itself transfer money; the payment rules determine actual amounts.
    Opponent edge probability
    Probability, from 0 to 1, that each initial undirected edge is assigned mutual opponent signs; otherwise both directions are allies. This is a sampling probability, so the realized fraction need not equal it exactly. Later loyalty updates can make the two directed stances differ. It does not control the fraction of opponent-preferring givers.
    Influence law
    Converts an actual payment P from a payer into signed influence on its recipient, after that round's loyalty update. Under Payment weakens repulsion, an ally has weight +P and an opponent has weight −w₀/(1 + P/w₀): payment weakens repulsion but never changes its sign. Under Wealth-scaled tanh / sign flips, weight is W × tanh(P/W − κ) for an opponent and W × tanh(P/W) for an ally, where W is the recipient's opening cash after interventions, before income and transfers. At W = 0, the weight is zero. No contact always means no influence.
    Opponent threshold κ
    A nonnegative, dimensionless payment-to-recipient-wealth threshold used only by tanh. An opponent repels below P = κW, is neutral at that threshold, and attracts above it. Allies use threshold zero. κ = 0 removes negative tanh weights. This parameter has no effect under attenuation.
    Signed pooling
    Incoming payer beliefs and the recipient's own belief are combined synchronously, using the signed contact weights. Negative pooling is projected to nonnegative mass and renormalized. This can set grid masses to zero. Bayesian evidence alone cannot revive a zero mass, although social input or a belief intervention can restore support. The model adds no probability floor. An unpaid contact with an opponent can still repel.
    Giving preferences & payment-dependent loyalty
    Ally-preferring & opponent-preferring fractions
    Initial probabilities for the two mutually exclusive giving-preference types. Each lies in 0–1 and their sum must be at most 1; the remaining probability produces a uniform giver. Realized agent counts fluctuate. Uniform givers choose uniformly among current neighbors; preferential givers use the preferred-group probability below. These are giver traits, distinct from edge signs and the independent loyalty trait.
    Preferred-group probability
    For a preferential giver, probability p of choosing its preferred ally/opponent group, with probability 1 − p for the other group, then uniform selection within the chosen group. A neighbor's stance toward the giver determines membership. Available groups with positive weight are renormalized: for 0 < p < 1, use the available group if the other is absent. At p = 1 only the preferred group is eligible; at p = 0 only the other group is eligible. No eligible neighbor means no contact or payment. The setting has no effect on uniform givers.
    Payment-dependent fraction
    Initial probability that each recipient has payment-dependent loyalty, sampled independently of giving preference. A positive actual payment makes such a recipient an ally of that specific payer immediately and resets that pair's unpaid clock. Unaffordable or zero payments do not reset it. Each payer–recipient direction has its own clock; initial signs are sampled normally and clocks start at zero.
    Unpaid rounds before defection
    A positive integer L. After L consecutive simulation rounds without positive payment from a particular payer, a payment-dependent recipient becomes that payer's opponent before social pooling on that Lth unpaid round. Timing starts from initialization or the latest clock reset, including a positive payment. Rounds without contact also count. This is a global duration for all payment-dependent recipients, not a total number of unpaid contacts. Recipient choice uses opening-round signs, before this round's loyalty changes.
    Payments, income & cash accounting
    Fixed payment c
    Nonnegative amount requested per payer per round under the fixed rule, in nominal money units. It is also the minimum requested amount under hybrid. It has no effect on the fractional or no-payment rule.
    Fraction f
    Fraction of the payer's opening cash W requested by the fractional rule, from 0 to 1. Hybrid requests max(c, fW). Opening cash includes interventions at that round but excludes income and incoming payments that settle later in the round.
    Payment rules & affordability
    Every experiment compares fixed (c), fractional (fW), hybrid (max(c, fW)), and no payment (fixed amount zero). Each agent contacts at most one eligible neighbor per round. A request above the available budget is skipped entirely, not reduced to the budget; otherwise the requested amount is paid. With no income, budget equals opening cash. A zero-payment contact can still cause opponent influence. No payment retains evidence, loyalty, income, and interventions; it is distinct from the baseline without interventions.
    Proportional income & budget / Income rate per round (r)
    The switch off sets r = 0. When enabled, the positive rate adds rW to each agent per round and limits each payment request to min(W, rW). For example, r = 0.1 gives income equal to 10% of opening cash per round. Requests exceeding this budget are skipped, not clipped. Income and incoming transfers cannot fund that same round's outgoing payment. Payments settle simultaneously and conserve cash; income and external cash interventions change the network total.
    Cash balance convention
    Cash uses arbitrary nominal units, without inflation or wealth normalization. Each round obeys: change in total cash = external cash adjustments + income. Income is computed on opening cash after interventions. A withdrawal cannot make an agent's balance negative. At zero total cash, wealth Gini is undefined and saved as JSON null.
    Agent intervention parameters & timing
    Selected agent
    The person whose behavior, cash, beliefs, or connection is changed, identified by its node ID. The intervention is applied to that same ID across every selected initial network and payment rule. Clicking a node in either comparison view pauses playback and selects it for editing; the baseline itself always omits the log.
    Effective from round
    A one-based update number within this experiment. Round 51 applies the change before update 51, preserving states 0–50. The default is the next round after the displayed state. Selecting an earlier round recomputes later states from the same inputs. At equal rounds, entries execute in their saved log order.
    Giving preference
    Choose uniformly, prefer allies, or prefer opponents. This assigns one person's giving type until changed again, using the global preferred-group probability. It does not change their neighbors' stances toward them.
    Payment-dependent loyalty
    Enabling this trait starts fresh unpaid clocks for the agent as a recipient and retains its current relationship signs. Disabling it freezes those signs against payment-dependent updates. Re-enabling starts fresh clocks; reapplying an already enabled setting preserves clocks. Connection interventions can still change signs.
    Add cash / Withdraw cash — Cash amount
    A positive amount applied once as an external addition or subtraction, not a transfer from another person. Multiple cash entries in the same round add in log order. A withdrawal that exceeds the agent's available cash in any intervened network/payment rule rejects the proposed run and identifies the failing scenario.
    Blend toward target — Target center θ
    Center of a Gaussian-shaped target on the existing θ grid, from 0 to 1. It is a Gaussian input parameter, not necessarily the normalized target's achieved mean, especially near boundaries.
    Target width
    Positive Gaussian width in θ units. Smaller widths concentrate mass more tightly; larger widths spread it across the bounded grid. Width is not necessarily the resulting discrete standard deviation. The target is normalized by summing its grid masses.
    Blend strength
    A dimensionless number a between 0 and 1: p_new = (1 − a) × p_current + a × target.Zero leaves beliefs unchanged; one replaces them with the target. The blend occurs once before evidence and social learning resume. Multiple same-round blends compose in log order; they are not averaged.
    Connections — Other agent & operation
    Choose a different agent and set ally, set opponent, or remove. Setting adds a missing connection or overwrites both directed signs of an existing one, resetting both unpaid clocks. Removing deletes both directions and their clocks; removing an absent connection is a no-op. Later payments can change conditional recipients' stances. An isolated agent still learns from evidence and receives configured income, but has no contacts or social influence.
    Persistence & shared randomness
    Behavior and connection assignments persist until later events or model dynamics change them; cash and belief edits act once. Payment amounts, preference probability, social rate, and loyalty duration remain global. No fresh random draws are made when editing: uniform givers retain prepared recipients when their neighbor set matches its initial state; otherwise stored contact uniforms select among sorted current neighbors. Preferential givers use those uniforms with current relationship groups. Actual contacts can therefore differ between matched runs.

    Reading the plots & metrics

    Node bars, belief plots, and heatmaps show discrete probability mass. Bars sum to one; lines between grid points are guides. Node mini-plots and belief curves share a fixed 0–1 mass scale; heatmaps use their labeled mass scale. The vertical truth marker is the generating bias θ*, not an estimate. Belief mean is the mass-weighted average of θ; within-agent SD measures the width of that person's belief. Population disagreement is the population standard deviation of agents' belief means, a different quantity.

    Network lines show current connections: solid for mutual allies, dashed for mutual opponents, and dotted purple for mixed directed stances. The allegiance table shows both directions. Under tanh, a contact's effective influence can differ from its relationship sign. Current opponent pairs counts links whose two directed stances are both opponent; agent degree counts the selected person's current neighbors. Node positions stay fixed during playback.

    Total cash sums every agent's balance. External cash this round is the signed sum of that round's injections and withdrawals; external cash to date is their cumulative sum, excluding income. Wealth Gini measures inequality, with zero for equal positive balances and values approaching one for strong concentration; it is undefined at zero total cash. Comparison-table changes are intervened minus baseline at the same displayed round.

    Paired runs, playback & reproduction

    Each round applies scheduled interventions, optional shared evidence, contact selection from opening signs, loyalty updates from actual payments, synchronous social pooling, and cash settlement. State t follows update t; its contact/payment ledgers correspond to that update. The baseline and intervened run share exact initial conditions and random inputs, the selected payment rule, node positions, and timeline. The baseline's intervention log is empty. With no interventions, the two views are identical; changed behavior or connections can later produce different contacts from the same draws.

    Network in focus chooses a graph family; Rule shown on nodes selects the payment rule for both networks and the baseline comparison; Selected agent chooses whose beliefs and cash to inspect. Play, step, scrub, and Speed change playback only. PMFs on nodes, Links, and Selected links only change visibility only. In the baseline belief overlay, dashed means baseline and solid means intervened. The separate payment-rule overlay, allegiance table, history, topology comparison, and agent gallery show the intervened run; its curve checkboxes affect only that overlay.

    Save experiment JSON preserves the setup, exact initial state and random inputs, ordered interventions, and model/dependency identity. Loading it with the matching implementation computes both runs. Save replay HTML embeds both trajectories for offline playback. Larger comparisons count both histories toward the export limit. These are exploratory individual realizations, not average effects across seeds; non-BA full-belief runs extend the earlier cash-only controls.
